Write the minutes of a Tactical Meeting of a circle, run according to Holacracy. The circle syncs on operational work and turns role tensions into concrete outputs: next actions, projects, information. Someone who missed the meeting reads these minutes instead of the recording: they must find every update and every tension, what was said about it and who has to do what.

Summarize, but leave nothing out. Go through the Transcript from start to end: every checklist item, metric, project update and agenda item gets its bullet, even a short one, and every discussion keeps its points (facts, figures, dates, names, reasons, objections, proposals, open questions) as bullets, without the back-and-forth. When unsure whether something matters, keep it in one line.

How to write it:

- Only the sections below, in this order, each with its `###` heading: no title, no other sections (no attendance, no check-in, no closing).
- Bullets, short sentences, no preambles. Sub-bullets for details.
- Write people by their name only, never with the "(Speaker N)" label the Transcript puts after it, and never as "Me". Add their role in brackets where the Transcript says it, e.g. "Mario Rossi (Secretary)".
- Leave out the check-in and closing rounds, greetings and problems with the call.
- Recognition gets words wrong: write the correct term when the context makes it certain (Holacracy terms such as "tension", "next action", "Rep Link", in Italian "cerchia" for circle, never "circolo"; tools such as "Holaspirit" or "GlassFrog"; role and project names as the personal notes or the invited participants write them).
- Numbers, dates and deadlines exactly as they are stated. When no deadline is stated, write nothing in its place.
- A phase that was skipped gets one bullet saying so and why; a section with nothing to put in it is left out, heading included. Never a line saying something is missing, not stated or not specified.

### Checklist Review
One bullet per recurring action read by the Facilitator: `Role: checklist item — CHECK` or `Role: checklist item — **NO CHECK**`, with what is said about it in a sub-bullet.

### Metrics Review
One bullet per metric: `Role: metric — value`, the value only if it is read out, with the change and the comments in sub-bullets.

### Project Updates
Only the projects read from the board in this phase, before the agenda is built; an agenda item is never a project update, even when it is about a project. One bullet per project: `Project — Role: update`, with the questions, answers and decisions on it in sub-bullets. If some projects are read out with no updates, one last bullet naming them.

### Agenda
One bullet per agenda item, in the order it was raised: `Label — who raised it`, including the items added during the meeting.

### Tension: Label
One such section for each agenda item, in the order it was processed, with the item's label; its owner is who raised it on the agenda:
- First bullet: `**Owner:** Name · **Need:** the tension in one sentence`, the answer to "What do you need?".
- Then the points of the discussion, in order, one bullet each: what is explained, every proposal and who makes it, the objections and doubts, the figures, the decisions. A discussion of several minutes gets several bullets.
- Then `**Outputs**`, one sub-bullet per output, without checkboxes (they are in the list at the end):
  - `Next action — Owner: what`, followed by `(deadline)` only if one is stated
  - `Project — Owner: outcome to achieve`, followed by `(deadline)` only if one is stated
  - `Information shared: what`
  - `Request for help — Owner: what`
  - `To Governance: the tension to bring there`

### Next Actions and Projects
Every next action and project agreed in the meeting, from the tensions and from the rest of the meeting (project updates included), as a checklist in the same format as the Outputs, starting with the type: `- [ ] Next action — Owner: what` or `- [ ] Project — Owner: outcome`, each followed by `(deadline)` only if one is stated. The owner is the role that takes it on when the Transcript names it, otherwise the person; never invent one.

### Tensions for Governance
Only the tensions someone explicitly says should go to a Governance Meeting, one bullet each: who brings it and the tension in one sentence. A process, a template or a task is not a governance change. Leave out this section, heading included, if nobody says so.

Write every heading, these ones included, in the language of the summary; keep "CHECK", "NO CHECK" and the role names as they are.
